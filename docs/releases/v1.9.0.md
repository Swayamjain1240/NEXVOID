# v1.9.0 — the exact cache, and the three ways it could have been wrong (M25)

**Tag:** `v1.9.0` · **Milestones:** M25 · **Requirements:** NFR-01, NFR-09 · **Plan:** [18](../18-performance-plan.md)

## What shipped

An exact answer cache in front of the answer path. The key is
`(tenant, query, mode, as_of, corpus_hash)` — every field is there because leaving it out would
produce a wrong answer rather than a miss:

| field | what it prevents |
|---|---|
| `tenant` | a hit served to a different tenant: a data leak, not a speedup |
| `as_of` | a 2024 question answered with the 2026 revision (M21) |
| `corpus_hash` | a superseded rule served after its document changed |
| `mode` | a sparse answer served for a hybrid question |

`corpus_hash` is a BLAKE2b fingerprint of the tenant's chunks (id, revision window, text),
computed once when the view is built, so invalidation needs no bookkeeping: old keys stop
matching. The cache is bounded (256 entries per process, `ATLAS_CACHE_SIZE=0` disables it) and
degraded answers are never stored — caching the fallback would keep serving it after recovery.

## Measured

`results/cache.md`, `python -m bench.cache`:

| state | requests | p50 |
|---|---:|---:|
| hit | 240 | **1.64 ms** |
| miss, cache off (control) | 264 | 2.39 ms |
| miss, cold (view warm) | 24 | 2.57 ms |

On a Zipf-weighted repeat mix over the 24 frozen questions, **216 of 240 requests hit (90.0%)**,
and `/v1/metrics` counted the same 216 hits and 24 misses the harness observed. The 1.5× is
smaller than M22's 2× on the retrieval path, and the report says why: at ~2 ms per request the
HTTP round trip and serialisation dominate what is left to remove.

The probes are the part that matters:

- **tenancy** — the same question string asked by two tenants was answered **75 percent** and
  **40 percent**, each from its own document, and each repeat hit its own entry;
- **the date** — the same question at 2024-06-01, 2025-06-01 and 2026-08-01 returned three
  distinct revisions, each repeat a hit on its own date;
- **invalidation** — re-ingesting the document turned a warm hit into a miss carrying the new
  figure.

## What else changed

- `Answer.cache` and a `cache` field in every answer body (`hit`/`miss`), so a cached response is
  visible to a caller instead of inferred from latency. A hit gets a fresh `trace_id` and reports
  `retrieve_ms` 0.0.
- `/v1/metrics` publishes a `cache` block: capacity, size, hits, misses, hit rate, evictions.
- The load reports run with `ATLAS_CACHE_SIZE=0`. Their workloads repeat 24 questions for
  hundreds of requests, so a warm cache would measure the cache rather than the server; the
  committed `results/bench.md` and `results/soak.md` numbers keep their meaning, and the reports
  say so in their notes.

## What it does not change

Frozen sets: recall@10 0.9167, refusal 1.0000, temporal 9/9 with 0 leaks, determinism 0
differences, contract 15/15. The eval path never touches the cache — it calls `answer_query`
directly, which is also why `bench/perf.py` still measures both retrieval arms.

## Carried forward

L20: the 90% hit rate belongs to an invented mix, and each worker process holds its own cache, so
a pool's effective hit rate is lower than any single worker's. The semantic cache was not
attempted — the exact cache already removes the repeated work, and a similarity threshold would
add tuning and a second way to leak. `docs/18` M26 (incremental indexing) is next.
