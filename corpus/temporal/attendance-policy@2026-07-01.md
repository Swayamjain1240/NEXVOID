# Attendance Policy

Minimum attendance requirements, condonation rules, laboratory requirements, medical leave and the
consequences of falling below the attendance bar. This revision took effect on 1 July 2026 and
raises the bar to 85 percent.

## Minimum attendance

Every student must maintain at least 85 percent attendance in each course, computed from the first
day of the term. A course is counted only if the student is on its current registration list;
attendance is not retroactively credited for courses added late.

## Condonation

Attendance between 75 and 84 percent may be condoned by a maximum of 10 percent, and only for a
certified medical reason. Condonation is applied once per course per term and is granted only when
the medical certificate covers the exact dates of absence. A student below 75 percent after
condonation is not eligible for a second condonation in the same term.

## Laboratory and workshop courses

Laboratory and workshop courses require 90 percent attendance. That requirement is absolute: it
cannot be condoned, no medical certificate reduces it, and the condonation clause above does not
apply to these courses. A student below 90 percent in a laboratory course is detained in that
course even when the overall attendance in every theory course is above 85 percent.

## Detention

A student below 75 percent attendance in any theory course, after all condonations, is detained in
that course and cannot sit for its end-term examination. A detained student must repeat the course
in a later term paying Rs 1000 as a course audit fee.

## Hostel priority

A student detained in two or more courses in one term loses hostel room priority for the following
term, and is allotted a room only after every student with a clear record has been accommodated.

## Medical leave

Medical leave of up to 15 days per semester may be sanctioned with a certificate from the campus
dispensary or a registered hospital. Anything beyond 15 days leads to a semester drop, processed by
the academic section and recorded on the transcript as a leave of absence.
